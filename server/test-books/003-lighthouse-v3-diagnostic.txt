"Listen," he says, and releases the stem. The tick spills out—tiny, brave, absurd against the ocean's slow accordion. Twelve seconds exactly, measured by gears that never heard of satellites. The watch ticks again. Below, the green begins to fray, pulled apart by a breeze tasting of rain and copper pennies. The creatures fold inward, becoming plankton again, becoming rumor. Alden closes the scuttle on their dissolution and fastens the latch with fingers that smell of kerosene and iron. He does not say her name. He does not say goodbye. He only winds the crank once more, restoring the twelve-second heartbeat that is obsolete, erratic, and—tonight—still alive.
